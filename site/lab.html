For the numbers people: our two models side by side with the bookmakers, and how accurate each one is.

This season's scoreboard

12 seasons of testing

We replayed 2014/15 to 2025/26 match by match. Each forecast used only results from before that match, as if we had made it at the time, and the model settings were chosen on earlier seasons. Accuracy is the Ranked Probability Score (RPS): 0 would be perfect and lower is better. "Base rates" ignores the teams and always predicts the league's usual share of home wins, draws and away wins.

Both models clearly beat guessing, and the goals model beats Elo. The bookmakers' closing odds are better still, in every season: they include team news and the views of professional bettors, while our models only know past scores.

How often each picked the right result

Hit rate: the share of matches where the outcome rated most likely actually happened. "Always home" picks a home win every time. Pure guessing would get about 33%.

Hit rate is easy to understand but a blunt measure: a 51% and a 90% favourite count the same when they win. RPS rewards getting the probabilities right, which is why we use it to choose and compare models.

Model details

Goals model (Dixon-Coles, 1997)

Home goals X ~ Poisson(λ) and away goals Y ~ Poisson(μ), with log λ = c + h + ahome + daway and log μ = c + aaway + dhome, where a is attack, d is defence (both centred on zero), h is home advantage and c the baseline. The joint probability is multiplied by a correction τ(x, y; λ, μ, ρ) for the scores 0-0, 1-0, 0-1 and 1-1.

Parameters maximise the time-weighted log-likelihood, with weight w = 0.5t / 270 for a match t days old (the half-life was tuned on 2008/09 to 2013/14), minus a ridge penalty pulling each team towards a prior (average, or weaker for promoted teams). Fitted with L-BFGS-B and an exact gradient, refitted before every match date on the previous four years of results.

Elo

Expected score E = 1 / (1 + 10−(Rhome − Raway) / 400). After the match both ratings move by K · G · (S − E), where S is 1, ½ or 0, and G is the goal-difference multiplier used by the World Football Elo Ratings. An ordered logistic model, refitted each season on earlier seasons only, turns the rating gap into home, draw and away probabilities.

Testing

Walk-forward only: every forecast uses data from strictly before its match date, and an automated test fails if any prediction changes when later results are altered. Settings were tuned on 2008/09 to 2013/14; 2014/15 to 2025/26 were never used for tuning. Bookmaker probabilities are 1 / odds, rescaled to remove the margin.
