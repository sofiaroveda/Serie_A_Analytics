How it works

How our predictions are made, how often they are right, and what Elo is. In plain English.

Reading a prediction

Each match has a bar split into three: the chance of a home win, a draw and an away win. Wider means more likely. After the match, the part that actually happened stays bright and the rest fades.

Home win Draw Away win

A 70% favourite should still lose or draw about 3 times in 10. That is not the prediction being "wrong": football is unpredictable, and good predictions are honest about it.

How our prediction works

Our prediction comes from a goals model (statisticians call it the Dixon-Coles model). It works in four steps.

  1. Rate every team's attack and defence

    From its results, each team gets an attack rating (how many goals it scores compared with an average team) and a defence rating (how many it concedes). An attack of 1.30 means 30% more goals than average; a defence of 0.80 means 20% fewer goals conceded. Recent matches count most: a match from nine months ago counts half as much as one played this week.

  2. Work out the expected goals

    Multiply the ratings together, with a small bonus for playing at home.

  3. Turn goals into every possible score

    Goals in football are rare, random events, and their count follows a well-known pattern (the Poisson distribution). From the expected goals we get the chance of every scoreline, with a small correction for low-scoring draws.

  4. Add them up

    Add up every score where the home side wins, every draw, and every away win.

What the model does not know: injuries, suspensions, new signings, a new coach, or a team resting players for a cup final. It only learns from scores, so it catches up with these after a few matches. The bookmakers know about them straight away, which is a big part of why they are slightly more accurate.

What is Elo?

Elo is a rating system invented by Arpad Elo, a Hungarian-American physics professor and chess master, to rank chess players in the 1960s. It is now used well beyond chess, including in FIFA's world rankings. We use it as our second model.

🔢

One number per team

Every team has a rating. An average Serie A team is about 1500; the best are around 1600 or more.

⚖️

Points change hands

After each match the winner takes rating points from the loser. Beat a stronger team and you take a lot; beat a weaker one and you take very few. Bigger wins move a little more.

☀️

Summer reset

Squads change over the summer, so each season every rating moves 30% of the way back towards average, and promoted teams start a little below average.

Our prediction record

Before each round, our predictions are saved to a public record on GitHub and never edited afterwards. GitHub keeps a history of every change, so anyone can check what we predicted and when it was published.
