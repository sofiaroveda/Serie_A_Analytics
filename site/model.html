How it works

Where our predictions come from, in plain English, and an honest look at how good they are.

Reading a prediction

Each match has a bar split into three: the chance of a home win, a draw and an away win. Wider means more likely. After the match, the part that actually happened stays bright and the rest fades.

Home win Draw Away win

A 70% favourite should still lose or draw about 3 times in 10. That is not the prediction being "wrong": football is unpredictable, and good predictions are honest about it.

Three ways to predict a match

⚽

Goals model

Our main prediction (known as Dixon-Coles). Every team has an attack and a defence rating, learned from its results, with recent matches counting most. It estimates how many goals each side will score, and so the chance of every scoreline.

📈

Elo ratings

The system used to rank chess players. Beat a strong team and your rating jumps; beat a weak one and it barely moves. Simple, but surprisingly good.

🏦

The bookmakers

Betting odds turned into chances (odds of 2.00 mean about 50%). The market knows about injuries and team news, so it is the benchmark to beat. Tap "Compare with bookmakers" under any match to see it.

How accurate are we?

We replayed 12 past seasons match by match. Each prediction used only results from before that match, exactly as if we had made it at the time.

Accuracy uses the Ranked Probability Score (RPS): 0 would be perfect, and lower is better. "Base rates" is a no-skill guess that ignores the teams.

Both our models are clearly better than guessing, and the goals model beats Elo. The bookmakers are better still, in every season. That is expected: their prices include team news and the views of professional bettors, while our models only know past scores.

Our prediction record

Before each round, our predictions are saved to a public record on GitHub and never edited afterwards. GitHub keeps a history of every change, so anyone can check what we predicted and when it was published.
